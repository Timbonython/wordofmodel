## 1. What the AdNews piece actually gives us

Source: AdNews, 28 August 2026, *Tourism Australia: AI leads $69 billion demand plan*.

The load-bearing sentence:

> "Progress will be tracked through **share of model**, the proportion of AI-generated trip summaries and recommendations that feature Australia."

**Tourism Australia is measuring share of model.** The national tourism body, in the first 59 years of its existence, has elevated machine visibility to a central marketing outcome — and named the metric almost exactly what this product was originally called.

Supporting facts, all attributable:

- Tourism 2035, a ten-year demand strategy, targeting $61–69 billion in annual visitor expenditure by 2035, up from $33 billion in 2025
- **"Marketing to humans and machines" is the first of four immediate priorities**
- $130 million carried under the federal THRIVE 2030 action plan
- CMO Susan Coghill: *"Around 40 per cent of travellers now use AI tools to help plan trips. So we are focused on ensuring that when someone asks an AI system where to go, Australia is in the answer."*
- Coghill again, and this is the sentence a small business feels: *"wanting Australia and booking Australia are two different things, and the path between them has changed significantly"*
- AI tools now 15% of digital sources for travel inspiration, level with online forums, behind search engines on 59% and social on 53%
- Research cited by Tourism Australia puts the rise in AI-referred traffic to travel sites at 2,215% since late 2024

**Why this beats the Semrush study by a mile:** it's a buyer adopting the category, not a vendor selling it. Quoting it sends nobody to a competitor. It's Australian. It's this week. And it's a $130 million organisation validating the exact problem a Bendigo dentist has for US$69 a month.

## 2. The naming question — Tim's call, and it's real

The metric was deliberately renamed **Share of Model → Recommendation Share** earlier this month, and redefined from *named* to *recommended*. That rename was right on the merits: being in the list and being the one endorsed are different things, and the second is where the money is.

Tourism Australia has now put "share of model" into the trade press with an institutional definition closer to *featured*.

Three options:

**A. Go back to Share of Model.** Rides the term, rhymes with Word of Model, and if the phrase catches on in Australian marketing circles then being the company named after it is worth a great deal. Costs the named/recommended distinction.

**B. Stay with Recommendation Share, ignore theirs.** Keeps the sharper idea. Leaves the term to somebody else.

**C. Use both, deliberately.** *Share of model* as the category — the thing Tourism Australia measures, the thing the industry is starting to name. *Recommendation Share* as what this product measures inside it, because being featured and being recommended are not the same and the difference is the whole product.

**I'd take C**, and it makes the article stronger rather than weaker. It lets Tim agree with the national tourism body and then say the sharper thing, rather than either following them or arguing with them. Agreeing and then going one step further is a much better look for a two-day-old brand than disagreeing with a $130 million agency.

If Tim takes A or B, the masthead byline and the report headings change with it, so decide before the writing page goes up.

### Shape

**1. Open on the fact, not the trend.**
Tourism Australia, 59 years old, has for the first time made being visible inside AI systems a central marketing outcome. Four immediate priorities and marketing to machines is the first one. $130 million behind it.

**2. And they gave it a number.**
Share of model. The proportion of AI-generated recommendations that feature Australia. Quote it exactly, attribute to AdNews, 28 August.

**3. Why that matters if you're not a national tourism body.**
Coghill's line does the work: wanting and booking are two different things and the path between them has changed. Every business has that gap. `[TIM: your own version of this in a category you know — the plumber, the dentist, whoever you've actually looked at.]`

**4. The bit I'd add to their definition.**
Featured is not recommended. Being in the list and being the one the model puts first are different outcomes, and only one of them gets you the booking. `[TIM: this is where the product's actual argument lives — say it plainly.]`

**5. What I actually do about it.**
The question your buyers type, run across the engines, and the answer back word for word. Who got named, who got recommended first, what got said about them that didn't get said about you. Not a score.

**6. What I don't claim.**
I can't tell you why a model said what it said. Nobody can. I can tell you exactly what it said and whether that changed since last month. `[TIM: this section is the one that makes the rest believable — don't cut it to save words.]`

**7. Close.**
Free scan, one question, about three minutes, no card. Ball back to them.
