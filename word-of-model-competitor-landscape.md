## The shape of the market

Two things worth saying before the table.

**Everyone sells a score.** The house style of this category is a number out of 100, a colour-coded dashboard, and a percentage of "AI visibility." That is a claim dressed as a measurement, and it is the single largest open position in the market. Refusing to score is differentiation that costs nothing to hold and cannot be copied by anyone whose product *is* the score.

**Every "thought leader" here is a vendor.** There is no neutral authority in this category to borrow from. Every study, index and report is published by someone selling the same thing. Which is why the Tourism Australia piece matters disproportionately — it is a **buyer** adopting the category, and quoting it advertises nobody.

## What the Tourism Australia piece does to this table

It puts a name on the category from outside the category. Nobody in the table above can claim a national body validated their metric, because until this week nobody outside the vendor set was using one.

**That is a genuinely time-limited advantage.** Every vendor in that table will find this article within a fortnight and will quote it. Being first to publish an argument on it — not just a repost, an argument — is worth doing this week rather than next.

## The two that need real research

Everything above is desk-level except the two Australian launches, and they are the ones that matter.

```
For Obsessd.ai and the ChatGPT/Copilot audit product, establish:
  - what they actually sell, in their own words from their own site
  - price, published or gated
  - do they show the raw answer, or only a score
  - subscription or one-off
  - who they are aimed at, and whether it is SMB or agency
  - who is behind them

Verified from their own pages, not from a directory listing or a
press release summary. Quote where you can.
```

Until that is done, this table is a sketch. It is good enough to decide positioning and not good enough to quote.

## The position that survives all of it

Three things nobody in the table above is currently claiming, and all three are true:

1. **No score.** The raw answer, word for word, and the trend in it.
2. **Australian.** Local categories, local competitors, local questions, priced in a currency the buyer is told about upfront.
3. **A person.** The premium tier is time with a human, and the founding cap exists because that human has a calendar. Nobody selling a dashboard can say that.

The first is the one to lead with. It is the only one that is a *refusal*, and refusals are memorable in a category where everyone is adding features.
